A new product. A market the giants already own. No budget to buy attention.

How a small team found its way in: map the market, pick one door, open the first partner and creator channels, test on paper before building. Nine months as growth marketing manager at a prediction-market startup.

  • A startup entering a crowded marketA prediction-market product, a small team, a runway, and bigger players who owned the coverage and the creators.

02 The situation

Bigger players owned the attention. A small team cannot buy its way in.

  • 170+ tools already served the users we wanted.Every niche had its own leader. Nobody was waiting for a newcomer.
  • Four steps from reading the news to a first trade.Leave the page, open an app, buy crypto, trade. Most people dropped off on the way.
  • No creator channel, and the big creators cost too much.The audience listened to people we could not afford.
  • Every month without users was a month of runway.The plan had to open doors, not describe them.
03 The thinking

Four paths on paper. Three rejected before a euro was spent.

Copy the leaders: they had a head start. Outspend them: not at our size. A third path stays private. The chosen one: five user segments, tools for outside developers, three phases with a gate each, six risks with six answers.

04 What was done

Six moves. Each one a document the team worked from.

Counts and structure only. Names, partners and internal numbers stay with the company.

research · 22 sections · 79 sources

Map the market first

Every tool the users already relied on, by category and by audience, and where the gaps were. 170+ tools reviewed; 168 went into the partner database.

partnerships · 15-tab workbook

Rank the partners, then knock

168 tools scored on seven points and narrowed to nine targets; 19 contacted. Outreach ran from the same file, with a follow-up date per row.

earned media · 14-section playbook

Earn the coverage

One argument base, five article briefs, four outlet tiers. A 30% placement rate was the floor; below it, the angle changes.

distribution · 3 routes

Put the product where people already read

A live market inside the article, with the crypto parts hidden. Three business tests done on paper before anyone built a thing.

platform strategy · 10 parts

Speak the professionals’ language

Thin markets scare professional traders away, and they will not rebuild their tools for a newcomer. So the platform speaks their software’s language. Four phases, nine risks, three partner tiers.

creator channel · from zero

Creators from zero, small on purpose

Small creators cost less and their fans trust them, so each test is cheap. 43 creators from three routes, 33 contacted. Contracts and UGC came out of this wave.
